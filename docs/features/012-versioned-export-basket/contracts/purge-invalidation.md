# Permanent-purge invalidation contract v1 (proposed)

Feature 005 has no permanent purge. Feature 011 is the authority for retention/recovery-copy decisions and original/XMP deletion. Feature 012 is a consumer; it does not add a public purge API, authorize deletion or merge 011.

## Durable handoff and fencing

An additive tombstone/outbox table records immutable event UUID, schema 1, owner/library, logical asset/edit scope, content category/checksum and affected storage versions when appropriate, approved purge operation/evidence ID, monotonic lifecycle generation, authorized actor/time, and cleanup acknowledgment. Match logical scope as well as content identity so deduplicated bytes retained by another eligible asset are not incorrectly invalidated. An original tombstone invalidates its dependent exports; an XMP tombstone invalidates only dependent rendered selections and their packages, not original-only exports. Manifest/audit retain safe non-sensitive identity and status, never original media or XMP payload.

Publisher and consumer share an owner/library lifecycle advisory lock (with documented ordering before request/row locks). Feature 011 validates its policy/retention/recovery-copy checks and writes authorized tombstone plus pending cleanup outbox under that lock before physical deletion. Selection, snapshot eligibility, worker dispatch, final package publication and download admission acquire the same lock and deny tombstoned scope. Replayed event IDs are idempotent; stale/foreign generations/scopes or conflicting evidence are rejected, never broadened to another asset/owner. No event may be inferred from an S3 404 or injected by an owner/operator endpoint.

In the same tombstone transaction, feature 012's invalidation consumer fences affected preparation attempts, marks dependent published packages unavailable, revokes all grants/bootstrap codes and creates durable cleanup jobs for package versions and temporary export versions. A worker deletes those exact versions (including noncurrent versions/multipart leftovers) and records acknowledgment; retry/failure remains observable. It does not delete shared feature 008 renditions used outside exports. Feature 011 owns final purge completion and must wait for export cleanup acknowledgment before claiming the derivative-removal obligation complete. Until acknowledged, deletion status remains draining/error; unavailable bytes remain inaccessible. Tombstones/audit survive consumer rollback.

## Writes admitted before invalidation

Database publication fencing alone does not prevent a previously started object upload from finishing later. Before any export-owned media write (temporary output, transformed derivative, ZIP, retry or multipart initiation), register a durable write intent under the lifecycle lock: owner/library/request/attempt generation, unique destination key/prefix, object category, writer identity, and admission state. An intent owns every object version created at that unique destination, even when the upload response/version or completion callback is lost. Record multipart upload ID as soon as available; prefix listing reconciles an initiation whose response was lost. No upload starts without an intent; storage adapters are the sole export writer path. Shared feature 008 renditions are outside export-owned intents and remain under their own lifecycle authority.

Tombstone closes new write admission under the same lock. Cleanup requests cancellation/draining of every admitted writer and keeps its intents until the writer's storage operations are confirmed finished/aborted and no retry can start. Lease expiration or a rejected API completion callback is not proof of storage quiescence. Unknown outcomes remain draining/error until writer shutdown/acknowledgment or equivalent verifiable storage fencing proves that no late operation can create media. Do not acknowledge cleanup merely after waiting a timeout. A late successful upload records its version on the intent independently of whether package publication is rejected; failed/lost callbacks and worker death trigger prefix/version/multipart reconciliation.

Only after proven quiescence enumerate all versions and multipart uploads at the intent-owned destinations, delete/abort them, verify no export media remains, and commit cleanup acknowledgment while admission stays closed. If proof, enumeration, abort or deletion fails, leave acknowledgment pending and alert/retry. Preserve intent/tombstone evidence until reconciliation is complete; rollback must not bypass it. Feature 011 cannot declare its derivative-removal obligation complete before this acknowledgment. This invariant covers objects never entered in a result/package row and every noncurrent version, without deleting deduplicated/shared renderer objects.

Mandatory fault tests pause an upload across tombstone and initial cleanup, then allow late upload success with a rejected/lost callback or worker loss. Cleanup acknowledgment must remain pending until writers are quiescent and all resulting versions/multipart remnants are removed; no media may appear after acknowledgment. Also test unknown multipart-initiation outcome, retry after invalidation, rollback during draining and failed deletion acknowledgment.

## Evidence and gate

Contract tests use a trusted in-process publisher fixture to exercise transaction/fencing behavior; they are labeled consumer-contract tests. Feature 011 must implement the authoritative publisher using this contract or an explicitly approved adapter with equivalent guarantees. T029 requires actual authorized source/XMP purge, denied exports/downloads, package-version cleanup acknowledgment, recoverable/displaced eligibility and publication/download races against that publisher. Synthetic fixture injection alone cannot satisfy AC-007, AC-013 or NFR-016 end-to-end evidence. Full feature completion and production rollout stay blocked until this prerequisite is met; independent 012 tasks may proceed after design approval.
